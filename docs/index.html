First launch: macOS will block it once. Here is how to open it

This is a free app without a paid Apple developer signature, so macOS says it “could not verify” it. That only means Apple has not reviewed it. It is not malware, and the source code is public on GitHub. Allow it once and it opens normally from then on.

  1. Click “Done”

    Open the app from Applications. When this dialog appears, click Done.
    Do not click “Move to Trash”.

  2. Click “Open Anyway” in Settings

    Go to Apple menu → System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to “Security” and click Open Anyway.

  3. Enter your password, click “Open”

    Enter your Mac login password and click Open once more. After that it opens like any other app.

Download and install

  1. Click Download for Mac below to get the .dmg (about 1 MB).
  2. Open the .dmg and drag Homescreen Organizer into Applications.
  3. On first launch, follow the three steps above to allow it.

Connect your iPhone

  1. Connect with a USB cable (Wi-Fi does not work) and unlock your phone.
  2. The first time, your iPhone asks “Trust This Computer?”. Tap Trust and enter your passcode.
  3. Click Read iPhone in the app and you are ready to go.

Privacy

Your home screen layout, app list and backups stay on your own Mac.

Only two things use the internet

  • App identifiers (like com.example.app) are sent to Apple’s public App Store lookup service to find each app’s category.
  • Clicking “Buy me a coffee” opens the Buy Me a Coffee page in your browser.

What it never does

  • No sign-up or login.
  • No collecting or uploading of personal data or usage.
  • No access to your photos, messages or anything inside other apps.

FAQ

Could it mess up my home screen?

Every read backs up your original layout. If you do not like the result, click “Restore Original Layout” in the sidebar to go back. You also get a preview before anything is applied.

Why does macOS say Apple “could not verify” the app?

Avoiding that warning requires a US$99/year Apple developer membership. This app is free and does not have one yet, so you need to allow it once by hand: see the steps. The source code is public on GitHub for anyone to check. If you chip in, the first goal is paying for signing so future versions open without the warning.

Which macOS versions are supported?

macOS 13 Ventura or later, on Apple silicon and Intel Macs. It is a native app written in Swift, so the download is only about 1 MB.

Is there a Windows version?

Not yet. macOS only for now.

Does it work over Wi-Fi?

No. It needs a USB cable, and your phone must be unlocked.

Does it support iPad?

It is designed and tested for iPhone only.

Why not a web or iPhone app?

iOS does not let websites or iPhone apps read or change the home screen layout. It can only be done from a computer over USB.

What if it stops working after an iOS update?

The app relies on connection methods Apple does not document, so an iOS update could break it. Please report problems on GitHub and I will update it as soon as I can.
